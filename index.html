How the analytics agent answers a question

Click through it with an example question.

A person asks in plain language

HR leader, in Microsoft Teams

What was headcount in Europe last quarter?

Short, natural, and ambiguous in a way that changes the answer. A plain text-to-SQL agent would pick a meaning and never tell you.

Every term is matched against the semantic model

What was headcount in Europe last quarter?

  • headcountResolved the Headcount measure, found in indexed metadata
  • last quarterResolved the most recent full quarter
  • EuropeAmbiguous where people work, or where their positions are based?

It asks instead of guessing

Agent

When you say Europe, do you mean where people work, or where their positions are based?

Pick an answer to keep going. Known trouble spots like location, cost groupings and reporting lines get a targeted question, and the answer carries through the rest of the conversation.

The question becomes a strict, validated plan

// QueryPlan, checked before anything runs
{
  "measure": "headcount",
  "time": { "grain": "quarter", "offset": -1 },
  "filters": [
    { "field": "work_location.region", "in": ["Europe"] }
  ],
  "clarified": ["Europe = where people work"]
}

The model's job ends here. Measures, time logic, filters and groupings live in a typed contract that can be validated and reviewed.

The query is written by code, not by the model

EVALUATE
ROW (
  "Headcount",
  CALCULATE (
    [Headcount],
    TREATAS ( { "Europe" }, 'Work Location'[Region] ),
    'Date'[Relative Quarter] = -1
  )
)

The same plan always produces the same query. It runs live against Power BI, and failures go through a repair loop instead of a retry-and-hope.

Nothing goes back until it passes review

  • Plan validated against the semantic model
  • Query ran, with row and evidence caps applied
  • A reviewer step approved the result, or it would have been held back
  • Run kept in encrypted storage, with tokens and timing recorded

The answer goes back with its methodology and data considerations, so the person knows exactly what was counted.

Mendly

Founder and sole engineer. October 2025 to now.

A two-minute daily check-in for therapy clients, made of questions their therapist chooses. The therapist sees how the week went before the next session and what lined up with the client's better days. I designed, built and run all of it: web, iPhone, Android, infrastructure and the business.

Visit mendly.me App Store Google Play

Rates, not averages

"Your mood averaged 6.3 on exercise days" is hard to feel anything about. Mendly says how often a day beat your usual instead, and only shows a comparison once it clears a statistical test. Every number is computed in code before a model writes a word about it.

Example figures, in the style of Mendly's own explainer.

Built for therapists

Per-client check-ins from 125 wellbeing metrics or custom questions, PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5 and AUDIT-C on a schedule, trend and correlation dashboards, and flags for a week worth a closer look.

One app, two kinds of account

A client invited by a therapist sees only the check-in. Someone using Mendly alone gets history, insights and an optional Plus plan. Separate identity pools keep the two apart, so a therapist never sees a personal account.

Language with guardrails

A Claude model on Bedrock writes the weekly read from facts it's handed, then a safety filter blocks diagnostic and causal claims and a crisis screen points to 988. No client clinical data ever reaches a model.

Private by design

Reminders never reveal what someone tracks or how they answered. Face ID and auto-lock, no ads or ad trackers, and sleep, steps and heart-rate variability from Apple Health and Health Connect.

Security and compliance

Role-based access across therapist, client and admin, MFA, CSRF protection, rate limiting, audit logging, versioned terms, data export and full account deletion, on HIPAA-eligible AWS services.
